01 / Capabilities

Delivery first, domain depth behind it

Programme leadership sits at the centre of what I do, supported by enough technical depth to make the decisions underneath it properly. These are the six areas I take responsibility for.

Programme management

Planning & critical path

Scope, sequence, dependencies and the handful of factors that genuinely determine the date. Risks visible to the people who can act on them, and decisions recorded as they are taken.

Technical

Enterprise API integrations

API orchestration, mobile SDKs, JavaScript tagging, REST integrations and SSO. Reading the integration directly means I can engage with engineering on the substance and judge what a change will genuinely cost.

Professional services

Client delivery at scale

Scoping, statements of work, onboarding against an SLA, governance cadence and handover. I have built the methodology as well as run it, including automating a scaled deployment task from twenty minutes to under ten seconds.

Communication

Executive reporting

Presenting to C-level and steering committees is a routine part of the job: the position, the decision being asked for, and the consequence of not taking it. No padding, and no surprises arriving late.

Recovery

Programme recovery

Work I am often asked to take on: an initiative that has been open a long time, where scope, ownership and sequencing have drifted apart. Re-establishing those three is a particular skill, and I have a record of doing it.

03 / Where I am heading

Applied AI in delivery

A developing focus, grounded in the automation and process improvement I have already delivered. The automation I built earlier was conventional engineering rather than AI, and the distinction is worth keeping. What carries across is the method: find where effort is being spent without return, design something repeatable, and measure whether it genuinely helped.

Delivery

AI-augmented programme work

Reporting, meeting synthesis, knowledge retrieval and stakeholder communication, with human review inside the workflow rather than bolted on after it.

Workflow

Agentic workflows, bounded

Multi-step workflows that connect information and routine actions, with defined permissions, escalation points and an accountable owner. The boundaries are the interesting part.

Operations

Measured adoption

Map where effort is going, prioritise by value and data readiness, run one focused pilot, then measure cycle time and rework before expanding. The measurement step is the one that determines whether anything was worth doing.

About

Technical depth. Commercial judgement. Accountable delivery.

I'm Eleftherios Kathijotes, a London-based technology and programme leader. I help organisations deliver complex implementations, recover initiatives that have lost momentum, and strengthen how delivery works once the immediate problem is solved.

My experience spans enterprise software, digital commerce, revenue operations and education technology. The sectors differ; the underlying problem does not. There is a significant customer, a technical integration with real consequences, several teams with competing priorities, and a commercial outcome that depends on all of it arriving together. What I bring is the ability to hold the technical detail and the commercial case in view at once, and to make the trade-offs between them explicit.

At Forter I lead technical delivery for strategic enterprise accounts, spanning integration design, payment authentication and coordination across product, engineering, analytics and the customer's own teams. Within my first eleven months I revived and completed a group of multi-year stalled projects, unlocking $1.5 million in revenue. That result came less from technical novelty than from establishing what each programme genuinely required, who owned the outstanding decisions, and what it would take to resolve them.

Earlier roles shaped that approach. At Coursera I delivered more than seventy enterprise projects and built automation that reduced a scaled-account deployment task from twenty minutes to under ten seconds, which mattered more as a method colleagues could apply consistently than as a saving in itself. At Instructure I led four implementation consultants and helped raise team customer satisfaction to 90%, which taught me that delivery capability rests on people and repeatable practice rather than individual effort.

My education supports both halves of the work. Computer science at Kingston gave me the grounding to read a system rather than take its behaviour on trust. Postgraduate study in management, innovation and technology at UCL gave me the commercial and organisational frame to judge what is worth building, and when an organisation is ready for it.

I am increasingly interested in how applied AI can improve the way delivery teams work: reducing administrative effort, making organisational knowledge easier to reach, and supporting programme coordination, with clear ownership and human review designed in. It is a developing focus, grounded in the automation and process improvement I have already delivered.

I also hold voluntary director appointments at Granville Square Ltd and South City Court RTM, two leaseholder-run companies in south London. I contribute to financial scrutiny, oversight of building improvement works, and coordination with managing agents and residents. Decisions rest with the board collectively, and the experience has sharpened how I think about governance and accountability.
