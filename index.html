01 / Capabilities

Delivery first, domain depth behind it

The discipline holds whether the programme is an API integration or a roof. These are the six parts of it I own.

Programme management

Planning & critical path

Scope, sequence, dependencies and the handful of things that actually determine the date. Risks tracked where people can see them, decisions recorded when they are made rather than reconstructed afterwards. PMP-trained.

Technical

Enterprise API integrations

API orchestration, mobile SDKs, JavaScript tagging, REST integrations and SSO. I can read the integration myself, which shortens most conversations with engineering considerably.

Professional services

Client delivery at scale

Scoping, statements of work, onboarding against an SLA, governance cadence and handover. I have built the methodology as well as run it — including automating scaled deployments from twenty minutes to under ten seconds.

Communication

Executive reporting

Presenting to C-level and steering committees is a routine part of the job: the position, the decision being asked for, and the consequence of not taking it. No padding, and no surprises arriving late.

Recovery

Stalled programmes

The work I am most often handed: a project that has been open for a year, where nobody agrees what is in scope and nobody wants to say so. Unpicking that is a specific skill and I have a record of it.

Property

Building works & governance

Specifying works, Section 20 consultation, tendering, appointing and holding managing agents to account, and reporting to a board of leaseholders who are spending their own money.

03 / Where I am heading

AI-enabled efficiency

An advisory focus rather than a track record, and worth being plain about the difference. The automation work behind me was conventional engineering, not AI. What interests me now is the same problem — how teams plan, coordinate and deliver — approached with tools that did not exist when I built the last version.

Delivery

AI-augmented programme work

Reporting, meeting synthesis, knowledge retrieval and stakeholder communication, with human review inside the workflow rather than bolted on after it.

Workflow

Agentic workflows, bounded

Multi-step workflows that connect information and routine actions, with defined permissions, escalation points and an accountable owner. The boundaries are the interesting part.

Operations

Measured adoption

Map the friction, prioritise by value and data readiness, run one focused pilot, then measure cycle time and rework before expanding. Most AI programmes fail at the fourth step.

About

Eleftherios Kathijotes

Principal Implementation Engineer at Forter, based in London. A career spanning enterprise SaaS, digital commerce, revenue operations and education technology — and, underneath all of it, the same job: connecting how systems actually work with what an organisation needs them to do.

A computer science foundation at Kingston, postgraduate study in management, innovation and technology at UCL, and no intention since of choosing between the two. The combination is the point. I can read the integration myself and still be the person holding the plan, which removes a translation layer that slows most enterprise programmes down.

The path ran through education technology first — Hult, then London Business School, then Instructure, implementing learning platforms for universities across Europe — before enterprise SaaS at Coursera and, now, payments and fraud at Forter. What stayed constant was the shape of the work: an enterprise customer, a technical integration, several teams who do not report to each other, and a date.

Most programmes I inherit are not failing on technology. They are failing because nobody has written down what is in scope, who decides, and what happens on the day it goes wrong. That is a systems problem before it is a software one, and it is the part I am good at.

Outside work I hold voluntary director appointments at two leaseholder-run companies in south London, where the same job arrives wearing a hard hat — expenditure scrutiny, works oversight, holding a managing agent to account, and explaining to residents why something costs what it costs. Unpaid, occasionally thankless, and the best governance training I have had.
